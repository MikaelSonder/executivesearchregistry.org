Executive search in Washington is the professional function through which organisations identify, approach and assess chief executive officers, C-suite executives, directors, independent directors and other senior leadership candidates for appointments where confidentiality, Washington market access, technology, aerospace, cloud, life sciences, maritime and clean-energy expertise, and structured assessment are material. The approach depends on the role, employer location, company state of incorporation, public or private status, sector and whether the mandate concerns retained search, Board renewal, CEO succession or a senior specialist appointment.

Washington does not operate a general state employment agency licence specifically for ordinary executive search. The profession operates within federal and state employment, pay, privacy, consumer reporting, corporate and sectoral rules. This state record therefore focuses on Washington’s distinctive hiring framework, particularly the Equal Pay and Opportunities Act, salary history restrictions, mandatory pay and benefits disclosures in covered job postings, wage discussion protections and the Washington My Health My Data Act where candidate or employee data includes consumer health information.

Washington law prohibits employers from seeking an applicant’s wage or salary history from the applicant or from a current or former employer, or requiring prior pay to meet a specified criterion. Employers with 15 or more employees must disclose the wage scale or salary range and a general description of benefits and other compensation in each job posting for a specific available position. These obligations can apply to postings that recruit Washington-based candidates, including remote roles. Search firms acting for employers should incorporate the controls into candidate outreach, role briefs and public or private search materials.

For international businesses, Washington executive search should be assessed together with the actual Washington work location or remote-worker connection, salary range and benefits disclosure, candidate privacy, Board and stockholder appointment authority, FCRA background screening, U.S. immigration and technology, aerospace, healthcare, financial services and other regulated-sector requirements. A search recommendation supports an appointment process but does not itself appoint a director, officer or employee.

The purpose of executive search is to identify, evaluate and present candidates for strategically significant leadership appointments where conventional advertising may be insufficient, confidentiality is needed or the candidate market is specialised. In Washington, a professional search process must also ensure that candidate outreach, role profiles, postings, compensation discussion and vendor instructions comply with salary history restrictions and, where applicable, wage range, benefit and other compensation disclosure rules.

Process Flow

Washington has no universal statutory executive-search process. A professionally structured mandate commonly moves from role, location, compensation and governance definition to market mapping, confidential direct approach, compliant candidate data and screening, shortlist presentation, authorised decision and formal appointment. Washington salary history and job posting disclosure controls should be established before candidate outreach begins.

1. Define the MandateAgree the role profile, employing entity, Washington work location or remote-work nexus, state of incorporation, reporting line, authority, sector requirements, wage scale or salary range, benefits, other compensation, mobility, confidentiality and search objectives.
2. Establish Governance and Compliance RouteDetermine whether the mandate concerns a director, independent director, chair, CEO, C-suite officer, senior executive, specialist or employee role; identify Board, nominating committee, stockholder, Washington pay, privacy, screening, immigration and sector-specific requirements.
3. Map the MarketIdentify relevant Washington, Pacific Northwest, U.S., North American and international companies, sectors, functional backgrounds and potential candidates, including passive candidates not actively seeking a move.
4. Prepare Posting and Candidate OutreachWhere a job posting is used for a covered position, include the wage scale or salary range and a general description of benefits and other compensation. Do not seek wage or salary history from the candidate or current or former employer. Provide appropriate candidate privacy information.
5. Assess and ReferenceConduct structured interviews and role-relevant assessment. Use references and background checks within an appropriate scope. Avoid collection of consumer health data unless a separate legal and privacy analysis confirms that it is necessary and permitted. Apply FCRA procedures when a consumer reporting agency is used.
6. Present the ShortlistPresent qualified candidates and assessment material to the authorised employer, Board, nominating committee or other responsible decision-maker.
7. Select and AppointComplete Board or stockholder process, executive contract, corporate filing, immigration and any regulated-sector suitability requirements. Maintain pay equity and privacy controls through final compensation discussions.
8. Onboarding and Follow-UpComplete candidate data retention review, privacy and vendor records, immigration and sector processes; provide follow-up or replacement arrangements where agreed.

Decision Tree

Executive search is an advisory and recruitment function rather than a single public approval process. In Washington, key decisions concern mandate suitability, Washington work location or remote nexus, salary history and pay disclosure, candidate privacy, Board appointment authority, FCRA screening and independent regulated-sector suitability processes.

Is the role senior, confidential, strategically significant or difficult to fill through open recruitment?If yes, assess whether a retained or exclusive executive search mandate is appropriate.
Will the candidate work in Washington, including remotely from Washington, or is the job posting directed to Washington applicants?If yes, assess Washington Equal Pay and Opportunities Act coverage, wage scale or salary range disclosure, benefits and other compensation disclosure, salary history prohibition and current remote-work guidance.
Will the search firm ask, research, verify or receive wage or salary history?Do not seek wage or salary history from the candidate, current employer or former employer, and do not require prior compensation to meet a criterion. Define the role’s compensation independently and avoid use of prohibited history.
Will candidate information include health, wellness, benefits or biometric-related information?If yes, assess whether the information may constitute consumer health data under the My Health My Data Act or trigger other privacy, employment, medical inquiry or vendor requirements. Collect only what is necessary and legally permitted.
Will a third-party consumer reporting agency provide a background report?If yes, comply with FCRA: provide standalone disclosure, obtain written authorization, certify compliance and follow pre-adverse and adverse action procedures before a decision based on the report.
Is the assignment for a Board, chair, CEO or senior executive role in an NYSE- or Nasdaq-listed company?If yes, establish the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee or independent director process, candidate independence criteria, Board succession needs, proxy disclosure and stockholder nomination or election route.
Does the candidate require U.S. work authorization or is the role in technology, aerospace, financial services, healthcare, life sciences, defence or another regulated sector?If yes, assess immigration, security clearance, export control, professional licensing, fit-and-proper, registration, notification or approval processes before final appointment.
Decision logic: Define the role, Washington work location or remote nexus, wage scale, benefits and appointment authority first. Then set salary history, privacy, screening, immigration and sector controls before market outreach. Only after the compliance framework is clear should the search firm begin sourcing, assessment and candidate presentation.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal risk is treating Washington executive search as a standard national sourcing process rather than a structured leadership, pay transparency, privacy, employment, governance and immigration process. Washington-specific compensation and candidate data controls should be embedded in the mandate and search-firm workflow before a candidate is approached or a role is posted.

Salary History RiskEmployers may not seek wage or salary history from applicants or current or former employers and may not require prior pay to meet a criterion. Search firms, references, public research and screening vendors must not be used to circumvent the prohibition.
Pay Transparency RiskCovered employers must include wage scale or salary range and a general description of benefits and other compensation in job postings for specific available positions. A confidential or retained search should still be assessed for posting and remote-work obligations before outreach begins.
Candidate Privacy RiskCandidate sourcing, assessment, reference checking, retention, vendor processing and cross-border transfers should reflect Washington privacy, data security and breach notification obligations as well as applicable federal and foreign law.
Consumer Health Data RiskCollection of health, wellness, medical, benefits or similar information can trigger separate analysis under the My Health My Data Act and employment, disability, genetic information and privacy laws. Do not collect information merely because it may be commercially interesting.
FCRA and Screening RiskWhen a consumer reporting agency provides an employment background report, failure to provide standalone disclosure, obtain written authorization or follow pre-adverse and adverse action requirements can create federal exposure.
Governance RiskFor public-company Board, chair, CEO and senior executive mandates, insufficient coordination with the nominating committee, Board composition, independence, succession, director checks and stockholder disclosure can undermine the appointment route.
Immigration and Sector RiskInternational candidates and appointments in technology, aerospace, defence, financial services, healthcare, life sciences, maritime and other regulated sectors can require separate visa, export control, security, licensing, fit-and-proper, notification or approval processes.

FAQ

Can a Washington executive search firm ask a candidate about salary history?No. Washington law prohibits an employer from seeking the wage or salary history of an applicant from the applicant or a current or former employer and from requiring prior wage or salary history to meet a specified criterion. The restriction should be applied to recruiters and other agents acting for the employer.
Must a Washington job posting disclose salary and benefits?For covered employers with 15 or more employees, each job posting for a specific available position must include the wage scale or salary range and a general description of all benefits and other compensation to be offered. Applicability, remote-work reach and current regulatory guidance should be checked for the specific posting and employer.
Does Washington law apply to a remote executive position?It can. Washington’s pay transparency framework can apply where a position may be filled by a Washington-based candidate, including remote work. The employer should evaluate the actual posting language, location, candidate eligibility and current L&I guidance before publication or outreach.
Can executive search collect candidate health or wellness information?Only with care and where there is a necessary, lawful and clearly defined reason. Health, medical, wellness, benefits or similar data can trigger separate issues under the My Health My Data Act, disability and genetic information laws, employment rules and privacy requirements. Routine executive search should avoid unnecessary collection.
Does this Washington page replace the United States national record?No. This page adds Washington-specific salary history, pay transparency, candidate privacy and consumer health data context to the U.S. federal overview. A mandate may also require analysis of the employer’s state of incorporation, other work locations, local law, federal law, exchange standards, immigration and sector regulation.
