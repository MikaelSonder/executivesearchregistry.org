Executive search in Bulgaria is the professional function through which organisations identify, approach and assess senior executives, Board of Directors members, Management Board members, Supervisory Board candidates and other leadership professionals for appointments where confidentiality, local market knowledge and structured assessment are material. The appropriate approach depends on role seniority, company form, ownership structure, sector and whether the assignment concerns retained search, CEO succession, board renewal or a senior specialist mandate.

Bulgaria does not operate a separate statutory licensing regime for executive search firms. The function operates within general contract, employment, equality and data protection law. Joint-stock companies can adopt a one-tier system with a Board of Directors or a two-tier system with a Management Board and Supervisory Board. Appointment authority depends on the structure selected and the company’s statutes: in a one-tier system, the general meeting elects Board members; in a two-tier system, the general meeting elects Supervisory Board members and the Supervisory Board appoints Management Board members.

The relevant framework includes the GDPR, the Personal Data Protection Act, the Labour Code, the Commerce Act and the National Corporate Governance Code. Candidate-data retention has a specific relevance in Bulgarian recruitment practice: the appointing authority must set a retention period for candidates’ personal data that may not exceed six months unless the applicant consents to longer storage. The National Corporate Governance Code supports transparent, competence-based nominations and committee practice for Board and Supervisory Board appointments.

For international businesses, Bulgarian executive search should be assessed together with the selected corporate governance model, Board or shareholder appointment route, Bulgarian-language needs, candidate-data retention rules, CEE market context, regulated-sector nomination and approval requirements and cross-border candidate-data handling. A search recommendation supports the appointment process but does not itself appoint a director, Management Board or Supervisory Board member.

Object Definition

Executive search in Bulgaria is the professional and advisory function concerned with identifying, discreetly approaching, assessing and presenting candidates for senior executive, Board of Directors, Management Board, Supervisory Board, leadership and specialist roles. The function commonly combines mandate definition, market mapping, confidential direct approach, competency assessment, referencing, succession planning and support for the client’s corporate and employment decision-making process.

Scope

The Registry Object covers the practical architecture of executive search and leadership recruitment assignments for organisations operating in Bulgaria. It focuses on retained mandates, market mapping, candidate sourcing and assessment, candidate-data handling, one-tier and two-tier governance structures, Board and Supervisory Board appointment context, Nomination Committee practice and the transition from a search recommendation to formal appointment.

Covered MattersRetained search mandates, CEO and senior leadership searches, Board of Directors, Management Board and Supervisory Board searches, market mapping, confidential direct approaches, assessment and referencing, succession planning, candidate-data handling and Nomination and Remuneration Committee practice.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains executive search as a recruitment and advisory process. It does not replace Bulgarian employment-law advice, individual contract negotiation, company-law procedure, board-structure analysis, shareholder appointment process or sector-specific suitability assessment.
Related but Not PrimaryGeneral recruitment, interim management, leadership assessment, executive remuneration advisory, employment law, corporate governance advisory, company secretarial services, tax and social-security advice, outplacement and relocation services may be connected but follow separate professional routes.
Outside ScopeVolume recruitment, temporary staffing, routine vacancy administration and general HR administration without a defined senior search, Board, Management Board or Supervisory Board appointment mandate.

Purpose

The purpose of executive search is to identify, evaluate and present candidates for strategically significant leadership appointments where conventional advertising may be insufficient, confidential handling is required or the relevant market is specialised. The function supports succession, leadership continuity and governance decisions, but it does not displace the legal and corporate responsibilities of the Board, Management Board, Supervisory Board, shareholders, owners or authorised employer.

PurposeTo identify, engage and assess candidates for senior executive, specialist, Board, Management Board or Supervisory Board roles before a formal appointment decision is made.
Business ValueStructured search can reduce appointment risk, support succession planning, extend access to passive candidates and assist decision-makers in assessing competence, leadership fit, independence, diversity and sector requirements.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Bulgarian executive search assignment is a qualified shortlist, assessment report or candidate recommendation. The final decision remains with the relevant authorised corporate body. In a one-tier joint-stock company, the general meeting appoints and removes Board of Directors members. In a two-tier joint-stock company, the general meeting appoints and removes Supervisory Board members, while the Supervisory Board appoints and removes Management Board members, subject to law and company statutes.

Primary OutcomeA shortlist, assessment report or candidate recommendation supporting the relevant appointment decision.
Decision BoundaryThe search firm may research, assess and advise, but the employer, Board, Supervisory Board, shareholders or other authorised corporate body retains responsibility for the appointment decision.
Appointment StepEmployment terms, senior management appointments, Board, Management Board or Supervisory Board appointments and any sector-specific suitability approval are completed outside the search recommendation itself.

Request Contexts

Executive search is normally initiated by a leadership gap, planned succession, Board competence need, confidential replacement, transformation project, acquisition, ownership transition or regulated-sector appointment. The search design should first establish the company form, selected governance structure, responsible appointment body, Nomination Committee role and any financial-sector or other regulated context.

Request ContextCEO or senior-management succession, Board or Supervisory Board renewal, confidential leadership replacement, private equity portfolio-company appointment, acquisition of a Bulgarian business, CEE regional expansion, industrial or technology transformation, financial-sector leadership appointment or a senior role in another regulated sector.

Country Characteristics

Bulgaria’s executive search environment is shaped by a concentrated senior market centred on Sofia, strong foreign investment, growing technology and business-services sectors, international manufacturing, financial services and broader CEE market links. A jurisdiction-specific feature is that Bulgarian joint-stock companies can adopt one-tier or two-tier governance. Candidate-data handling also has a practical statutory retention rule: data from a staff selection procedure may generally be kept no more than six months unless the applicant has consented to longer storage.

Operational CultureRelationship-aware, confidentiality-led and increasingly international. Credible sector knowledge, Bulgarian-language capability, clear mandate definition and a disciplined assessment process are important in senior appointments.
Institutional StructureNo single regulator oversees executive search. Relevant obligations arise under data protection, employment and equality law, while corporate appointments may involve Boards, Management Boards, Supervisory Boards, shareholders, Nomination Committees and sector regulators.
Governance LogicThe appointment route depends on whether a joint-stock company uses a one-tier or two-tier system. The National Corporate Governance Code recommends transparent nomination and election processes, candidate competence and independence assessment, and committee structures. In significant banks, Nomination Committees have detailed statutory regulatory functions relating to Board and senior-management selection.
Language ExpectationBulgarian is commonly important for domestic leadership, employee-relations, regulatory, Board and stakeholder roles. English is common in multinational and technology businesses, but role-specific Bulgarian capability should be assessed rather than assumed.

Process Flow

Bulgaria has no universal statutory executive-search process. A professionally structured mandate commonly moves from role and governance definition to market mapping, confidential direct approach, assessment, shortlist presentation, authorised decision and formal appointment. For Board, Management Board and Supervisory Board roles, the process should be aligned with the company’s one-tier or two-tier model, shareholder route, committee practice and any regulated-sector requirements.

1. Define the MandateAgree the role profile, legal entity, business and ownership context, authority, reporting line, required expertise, leadership criteria, remuneration parameters, Bulgarian-language needs and confidentiality requirements.
2. Establish the Governance RouteDetermine whether the mandate concerns a Board of Directors, Management Board, Supervisory Board, senior executive, specialist or employee role; identify the selected governance model, authorised decision-maker, committee role, company statutes and sector-specific requirements.
3. Map the MarketIdentify relevant Bulgarian, CEE, European and international companies, sectors, functional backgrounds and potential candidates, including passive candidates not actively seeking a move.
4. Approach CandidatesMake confidential and proportionate contact, provide appropriate information concerning personal-data processing and explain the opportunity to the extent permitted by the mandate.
5. Assess and ReferenceConduct structured interviews, assess role-relevant experience, competence, independence, diversity and language capability where applicable, obtain lawful and proportionate reference information and use agreed assessment tools where appropriate.
6. Present the ShortlistPresent qualified candidates and assessment material to the authorised employer, Board, Supervisory Board, Nomination Committee or other responsible decision-maker.
7. Select and ApproveThe client completes the relevant internal route, including Board action, shareholder appointment process, Supervisory Board decision or sector-specific suitability and approval step.
8. Offer and AppointmentAgree terms and formalise the employment, senior management, Board, Management Board or Supervisory Board appointment under the relevant contractual and company-law framework.
9. Onboarding and Follow-UpSupport transition and, where agreed, provide follow-up or replacement arrangements under the search engagement.

Decision Tree

Executive search is an advisory and recruitment function rather than a public approval process. The key decisions concern mandate suitability, the applicable corporate appointment route, candidate-data processing and retention, Nomination Committee involvement and any independent regulated-sector suitability process.

Is the role senior, confidential, strategically significant or difficult to fill through open recruitment?If yes, assess whether a retained or exclusive executive search mandate is appropriate.
Does the company use a one-tier or two-tier governance structure?In a one-tier system, map Board of Directors and shareholder authority. In a two-tier system, map the Management Board, Supervisory Board and shareholder appointment route before defining the search process.
Is the appointment in a listed issuer or significant bank?If yes, establish the Nomination Committee’s role, candidate competence, independence, diversity and time-commitment criteria, and any approval or nomination procedure that applies.
Will candidate data be retained after the current search process?If yes, determine the retention period. Candidate data from a staff selection procedure generally may not be retained longer than six months unless the applicant consents to a longer period or special law provides otherwise.
Will candidate data be sourced from third parties or transferred internationally?If yes, establish the GDPR and Bulgarian-law basis, transparency information, data-minimisation approach and applicable transfer safeguards before processing.
Decision logic: First define the role and identify the corporate body with appointment authority. Then establish the search mandate, governance route, candidate retention approach and any regulated-sector process. Candidate mapping and confidential outreach should begin only once these parameters are clear.

Cross-border candidate pools are often relevant, but the search and appointment route must remain aligned with Bulgarian corporate, employment, data-protection, language and sector-specific requirements.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal risk is treating executive search as a simple sourcing activity rather than a structured leadership, governance and data-protection process. Weak mandate definition, failure to identify the selected corporate model, inappropriate candidate-data retention, inadequate candidate-suitability evidence or poor coordination with Board and Nomination Committee procedures can affect appointment quality, timing and legal exposure.

Role Definition RiskAn unclear, changing or internally inconsistent profile can produce an unsuitable shortlist, delay the process and weaken candidate engagement.
Governance Structure RiskFailing to identify whether the company uses a one-tier or two-tier system can result in a search process misaligned with the actual Board, Supervisory Board and shareholder appointment authority.
Candidate Retention RiskRetaining candidate personal data beyond the permitted period for a current selection procedure without valid consent or another applicable legal basis can create data-protection exposure.
Candidate Suitability RiskFor Board and Supervisory Board appointments, insufficient assessment of professional skills, knowledge, qualifications, independence, diversity and expected time commitment can weaken the appointment process.
Data Protection RiskCollecting, retaining, assessing or disclosing candidate information without an appropriate GDPR and Bulgarian-law basis, transparency process and retention approach may create significant exposure.
Sector RiskAppointments in banking, financial services and other regulated sectors may require independent fit-and-proper, nomination, notification or approval steps outside the executive-search mandate.

FAQ

Is executive search a regulated profession in Bulgaria?No. Bulgaria does not operate a dedicated licensing regime for executive search firms. The function is shaped by general data-protection, employment, equality and contract law, together with company-law, corporate-governance and sector-specific requirements where relevant.
Do Bulgarian joint-stock companies use the same governance structure?No. A Bulgarian joint-stock company may adopt a one-tier system with a Board of Directors or a two-tier system with a Management Board and Supervisory Board. The selected structure and the company’s statutes should be checked before designing a senior search or appointment process.
Who appoints Management Board members in a two-tier company?In a two-tier joint-stock company, the Supervisory Board appoints and removes Management Board members. Supervisory Board members are appointed and removed by the general meeting of shareholders under the applicable company-law framework.
How long may candidate data be retained in Bulgarian recruitment?The appointing authority must determine a retention period for personal data collected in a staff selection procedure. It may generally not exceed six months unless the applicant has consented to longer storage or a special law provides otherwise. Once the period expires, the documents should be erased or destroyed unless another law requires retention.
Does GDPR apply to executive search candidate data?Yes. Search firms and employers must process candidate data under an appropriate legal basis, apply transparency, data minimisation and retention controls, and use safeguards for international transfers where applicable.
