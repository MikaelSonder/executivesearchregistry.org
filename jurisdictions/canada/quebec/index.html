Executive search in Québec is the professional function through which organisations identify, approach and assess chief executive officers, executive and independent directors, chairs, C-suite executives and other senior leadership candidates. It is used where confidentiality, Québec, Canadian and international market access, Francophone leadership capability, sector knowledge, Board succession and structured assessment are material. Québec’s executive market includes Montréal and other centres across financial services, technology, artificial intelligence, life sciences, aerospace, industrial, consumer, professional services, education, culture, public institutions and international business.

Québec distinguishes personnel placement agencies from temporary foreign worker recruitment agencies. A personnel placement agency is an enterprise, organisation, partnership or person that offers personnel placement services, including as an ancillary activity. A placement agency provides workers’ services to a client enterprise to meet staffing needs. Such agencies require a CNESST permit to operate in Québec. By contrast, a retained executive search firm that only introduces candidates for direct employment and does not provide personnel placement services may fall outside the personnel placement agency permit definition; however, the actual service model should be assessed carefully, especially where the firm supplies workers, recruits temporary foreign workers or undertakes combined search and staffing activity.

French is the official and common language of Québec, and recruitment processes must reflect the Charter of the French Language. Job postings must be available in French and, where posted in another language, made available simultaneously with the same means of transmission and to a proportionally comparable target audience. An employer may require knowledge of a language other than French only where the nature of the duties requires it, after taking all reasonable means to avoid imposing that requirement. Executive search must therefore distinguish a genuinely necessary non-French requirement from an unsupported preference.

Candidate information is governed principally by Québec’s Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, as modernised by Law 25, with separate public-sector privacy legislation for public bodies. Candidate research, sourcing, reference checks, screening, assessment, retention, cross-border transfers and AI-supported processes require a Québec-specific privacy analysis. A search recommendation supports, but does not replace, formal Board, shareholder, corporate filing, immigration, temporary foreign worker, securities or sector-specific appointment processes.

Object Definition

Executive search in Québec is the professional and advisory function concerned with identifying, confidentially approaching, assessing and presenting candidates for chair, Board, chief executive officer, executive director, C-suite, senior specialist and regulated leadership roles. It commonly combines mandate definition, market mapping, direct approach, competency assessment, reference and background process design, candidate data management, succession planning and support for the client’s Board, shareholder and employment decision-making processes.

Purpose

The purpose of executive search is to identify, evaluate and present candidates for strategically significant leadership appointments where conventional advertising may be insufficient, confidentiality is needed or the candidate market is specialised. In Québec, the search design should establish the actual role location, employer and service model, placement agency permit position, French-language approach, human-rights and privacy framework, candidate screening process, Board authority and immigration or sectoral requirements before substantive candidate outreach begins.

PurposeTo identify, engage and assess candidates for senior executive, specialist, CEO, Board, chair, C-suite or Québec leadership roles before a formal appointment decision is made.
Business ValueStructured search can reduce appointment risk, support Board and CEO succession, extend access to Québec, Canadian and global passive candidates and help decision-makers manage permits, French-language obligations, privacy, human rights, immigration, governance and regulated-sector requirements.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Québec executive search assignment is a qualified shortlist, assessment report or candidate recommendation. The final decision remains with the relevant authorised employer, Board, nominating committee or shareholders. Director appointment depends on the organisation’s incorporating statute, articles, by-laws, shareholder agreements and securities or exchange requirements. The formal route can be governed by the Canada Business Corporations Act, Québec Business Corporations Act or another incorporating statute and must be confirmed before the search concludes.

The provincial regime for personnel placement agencies and temporary foreign worker recruitment agencies, together with the Charter of the French Language, makes early service-model and language-process classification essential.

Process Flow

Québec has no universal statutory executive-search timetable. A professionally structured mandate moves from role, employer, service-model, permit, French-language and governance definition to market mapping, confidential direct approach, assessment, compliant screening, shortlist presentation, authorised decision and formal appointment. The service and permit classification, language workflow and privacy design should be resolved before active candidate outreach or public advertising begins.

1. Define the MandateAgree the role profile, employing entity, Québec work location, reporting line, authority, sector requirements, compensation, French-language and other language needs, mobility, confidentiality and search objectives.
2. Confirm Service Model and Permit StatusDetermine whether the provider offers personnel placement services, recruits temporary foreign workers or only introduces candidates for direct employment. If the statutory placement or temporary foreign worker recruitment definitions apply, obtain or verify the required CNESST permit before operating.
3. Establish French-Language, Equality and Privacy FrameworkPrepare French role, job-posting and candidate communication materials. Where another language is proposed as a requirement, document necessity and reasonable measures taken to avoid it. Identify privacy governance, notice, necessary information, retention, confidentiality, vendor, AI and cross-border transfer controls.
4. Establish Governance and Regulatory RouteDetermine whether the mandate concerns a director, independent director, chair, CEO, C-suite officer, senior executive, specialist, public officeholder or regulated role; identify Board, nominating committee, shareholder, corporate statute, securities, immigration, professional and sector-specific requirements.
5. Map the MarketIdentify relevant Québec, Canadian, North American and international companies, sectors, functional backgrounds and potential candidates, including passive candidates not actively seeking a move.
6. Approach CandidatesMake confidential and proportionate contact, provide appropriate French and, where relevant, other-language candidate process and privacy information, explain the opportunity accurately and apply language, accommodation and human-rights criteria only where objectively relevant.
7. Assess and VerifyConduct structured interviews and role-relevant assessment. Obtain or verify references, professional registration, work authorisation, criminal-record, credit, security, financial-services, public-sector or other regulated information only where the role and legal framework support it.
8. Present the ShortlistPresent qualified candidates and assessment material to the authorised employer, Board, nominating committee or other decision-maker, using data minimisation and controlled disclosure.
9. Select and AppointComplete offer, executive agreement, Board or shareholder process, director or officer filing, securities disclosure, Québec and federal work authorisation and any banking, insurance, public-sector or sector-specific suitability requirement.
10. Onboarding and Follow-UpComplete candidate-data retention review, CNESST permit records, French-language documentation, appointment records, immigration and regulatory submissions, transition support and any replacement guarantee process under the engagement agreement.

Decision Tree

Executive search is an advisory and recruitment function rather than a single public approval process. In Québec, the principal early decisions concern the actual service model, CNESST permit status, French-language job requirements and posting process, Law 25 privacy, Board authority, work authorisation and independent regulated-sector suitability processes.

Is the role senior, confidential, strategically significant or difficult to fill through open recruitment?If yes, assess whether a retained or exclusive executive search mandate is appropriate.
Does the provider supply workers’ services to a client enterprise to meet labour needs, or recruit temporary foreign workers?If yes, assess the Québec personnel placement agency or temporary foreign worker recruitment agency definitions and obtain the appropriate CNESST permit before operating. A firm providing only direct-hire executive introduction should still document its classification and review combined service models carefully.
Will the role be advertised, posted or otherwise publicly communicated in Québec?If yes, ensure that the opportunity is available in French. If another language version is used, publish it simultaneously using the same means and to a proportionally comparable target audience. Align employment documents and candidate communications with the Charter of the French Language.
Will a language other than French be required?If yes, document why the nature of the duties requires it and the reasonable measures taken to avoid imposing the requirement. Do not use a non-French language requirement as an unsupported preference or proxy for national origin, ethnicity or other protected ground.
Will candidate data be collected from public profiles, references, third parties, through AI, across provincial boundaries or from outside Canada?If yes, establish the Québec privacy framework, transparency, necessity, privacy officer, lawful authority or consent, security, retention, service-provider controls and cross-border privacy impact assessment before sourcing and client presentation.
Is the assignment for a Board, chair, CEO or senior executive role in a reporting issuer or exchange-listed company?If yes, establish the nominating or governance committee process, candidate independence and skills criteria, Board succession needs, corporate statute, articles, by-laws, securities disclosure and shareholder nomination or election route.
Does the candidate require Canadian or Québec work authorisation or is the role in banking, insurance, aerospace, healthcare, education, public sector, defence or another regulated sector?If yes, assess federal and Québec immigration, temporary foreign worker recruitment, professional registration, security, financial-services, fit-and-proper, notification or approval processes before final appointment.
Decision logic: Define the role, Québec work location, service model, permit position, French-language requirements, data processing and appointment authority first. Then establish placement, language, privacy, human-rights, immigration, Board and sector-specific routes before candidate mapping and confidential outreach begin.

Cross-Border Relevance

Québec executive search is strongly cross-border. Montréal is a major Francophone and North American centre for aerospace, AI, technology, finance, life sciences, culture, professional services and global corporate leadership. Québec businesses recruit leaders from France, Europe, the United States, Africa, Latin America and other markets, while Québec executives are frequently considered for international appointments. Global search mandates need a Québec framework for French, CNESST permit status, privacy, immigration, temporary foreign worker recruitment, human rights, Board governance and sectoral requirements.

RecognitionExecutive search is a professional advisory and recruitment function rather than a separately licensed Québec professional title. However, personnel placement or temporary foreign worker recruitment activity can require a CNESST permit, and the actual work location, service function and candidate geography determine the relevant route.
Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned businesses recruiting into Québec should align global search processes with Québec placement agency, French-language, employment, human-rights, privacy, immigration, corporate and sector-specific requirements. A Québec subsidiary, branch, employer of record or foreign employer can each require different analysis.
Language ConsiderationsFrench is Québec’s official and common language. Québec recruitment materials and job postings must be available in French, and any non-French posting must follow simultaneous and comparable publication rules. English or another language may be required only where necessary to perform the duties after reasonable efforts to avoid that requirement have been taken.
International RulesQuébec placement permit rules, Charter of the French Language, Québec privacy law, federal and Québec immigration law can apply. GDPR, US state privacy, UK GDPR and other foreign regimes may also apply to candidate information where territorial scope is met. Cross-border personal information communication requires appropriate Québec privacy assessment and safeguards.
Practical ConsiderationsAssignment planning may need to address Québec work location, service model and CNESST permit, French-language job materials, non-French language justification, candidate data transfer, work authorisation, Québec Acceptance Certificate or other immigration route where relevant, tax and social security, executive contracts, Board and shareholder procedure, securities disclosure and regulated-sector approval.
Typical RisksAssuming a retained-search label removes placement-agency permit exposure, publishing an English-only Québec job opportunity, requiring English without a documented operational need, transferring candidate information outside Québec without an appropriate assessment, treating a foreign candidate as automatically work-authorised, or using a global candidate database without transparent, lawful and secure processing.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal risk is treating Québec executive search as a standard Canadian sourcing process rather than a service-model, permit, French-language, privacy, human-rights, governance and immigration process. The client and search firm should identify the actual work location, candidate geography, employer type and search model before deciding what may be advertised, requested, shared or done.

CNESST Permit RiskOffering personnel placement services or temporary foreign worker recruitment without the appropriate CNESST permit can create enforcement, contractual and client-side risk. The service should be assessed by its substance, including whether workers’ services are supplied to client enterprises, not by the executive-search label alone.
French-Language RiskPublishing or communicating a Québec opportunity without the required French availability, failing to publish non-French versions simultaneously and comparably, or imposing English or another language requirement without necessity and reasonable avoidance measures can create language-law and employment risk.
Privacy and Cross-Border Data RiskCandidate sourcing, assessment, reference checking, background reports, AI use, retention, vendor processing and international transfers can trigger Québec privacy law. Public availability of a professional profile does not eliminate transparency, necessity, governance, security and cross-border assessment obligations.
Human Rights and Accommodation RiskRole criteria, candidate outreach, advertisements, interviews and selection should be objectively connected to the work and incorporate applicable accommodation duties. Language, credential, employment-history and screening standards should not be used as proxies for protected characteristics.
Screening RiskCriminal-record, credit, medical, social-media, professional and consumer-reporting checks should be role-related, proportionate, accurate and obtained under the appropriate Québec, federal, privacy, human-rights and sector-specific framework.
Governance and Securities RiskFor Board, chair, CEO and senior executive mandates, insufficient coordination with the nominating committee, Board composition, independence, succession, corporate statute, articles, by-laws, securities disclosure and shareholder process can undermine the appointment route.

FAQ

Does an executive search firm need a CNESST permit in Québec?It depends on the actual service. Québec personnel placement agencies and temporary foreign worker recruitment agencies need a CNESST permit. A personnel placement agency provides workers’ services to a client enterprise to meet staffing needs. A direct-hire retained executive search firm that only introduces candidates may not be a personnel placement agency, but it should document its service classification and review any temporary foreign worker recruitment or combined staffing activity carefully before operating.
What security is required for a Québec personnel placement agency permit?CNESST states that a personnel placement agency permit application must be accompanied by 15,000 Canadian dollars security to guarantee performance of certain obligations. The security must remain valid for the permit’s duration. Current permit fees, security form, Revenu Québec attestation and other conditions should be verified directly with CNESST.
Must an executive-search job posting be in French in Québec?Yes. Québec job postings must be available in French. If a version in another language is also posted, it must be made available simultaneously, using the same means of transmission, and reach a proportionally comparable target audience. Recruitment and employment documentation should be designed around the Charter of the French Language.
Can an employer require English for a Québec executive role?Only where knowledge of English or another language is necessary for the duties. The employer must first take all reasonable means to avoid imposing that requirement and should document the operational justification, such as unavoidable client, supplier, safety, legal or international responsibilities. A general preference for English is not sufficient.
Does Law 25 apply to executive candidate data?Québec’s private-sector privacy law, as modernised by Law 25, can apply to candidate research, sourcing, CVs, assessments, references, screening, retention, vendor use, AI and cross-border data transfers. The organisation should establish transparent and necessary processing, privacy governance, security and, where required, a privacy impact assessment before communicating personal information outside Québec.
Does a search recommendation appoint a Québec company director?No. A search recommendation is advisory. The director appointment must follow the applicable Québec or federal corporate statute, the company’s articles and by-laws, authorised Board or shareholder procedure, director consent, corporate filing and any securities or regulated-sector requirements.
